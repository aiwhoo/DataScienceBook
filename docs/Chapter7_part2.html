Data Storytelling & Insight Presentation

Part 2: Presentation & Peer Feedback

Author

Technical Instructional Team

Learning Objectives

By the end of this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Give constructive feedback on presentations.
  • Identify strengths and weaknesses in communication.
  • Build a focused two-slide presentation.
  • Recognize the characteristics of strong data presentations.
  • Communicate insights clearly and effectively.

Introduction: From Analysis to Communication

In Part 1, we explored the foundations of data storytelling.

In this section, we focus on applying those skills through presentation design, peer critique, and communication practice.

Professional data scientists do more than analyze data — they also explain findings clearly and effectively.

NoteThe Big Idea

Strong presentations improve through feedback, revision, and practice.

Peer Critique: Giving Helpful Feedback

When reviewing a classmate’s presentation, focus on three important questions.

What Is Clear?

Identify what works well and what the audience can understand easily.

What Is Confusing?

Look for anything that feels unclear, overly technical, or difficult to follow.

What Could Improve?

Offer one specific and actionable suggestion.

Good feedback should help presenters improve clarity and communication.

Why Feedback Matters

Peer critique helps presenters:

  • Improve clarity
  • Identify missing explanations
  • Strengthen organization
  • Refine their message

Fresh perspectives often reveal problems the presenter may not notice on their own.

TipInstructor Insight

Critique is not about criticism — it is about helping others communicate more effectively.

Build a Two-Slide Presentation

In this activity, students create a short presentation using their own research or dataset.

The goal is to communicate one clear and meaningful insight.

Slide 1: Set the Stage

The first slide introduces the topic and provides context.

Include:

  • Your research question
  • Brief background information
  • Why the topic matters

The audience should quickly understand why they should care.

Slide 2: Deliver the Insight

The second slide focuses on the main finding.

Include:

  • The most important insight
  • A key takeaway
  • Why the finding matters

Every element on the slide should support one central message.

What Makes a Strong Presentation?

Strong presentations are:

Clear

The audience understands the message quickly.

Focused

The presentation centers around one major idea.

Insightful

The presentation explains why the findings matter.

Story-Driven

The visuals and data support a meaningful narrative.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Too Much Information

Overcrowded slides can confuse audiences.

Weak Visual Design

Charts should be simple and easy to read.

No Clear Message

Every presentation should communicate one important takeaway.

Presenting Data Without Context

Data alone is not enough — explain why it matters.

Professional Presentation Tips

Focus on One Insight

If the audience remembers one thing, what should it be?

Keep Visuals Simple

Remove unnecessary labels, gridlines, and clutter.

Make the Message Obvious

Do not make audiences search for the point.

Tell a Story

A strong presentation moves logically from: * Context * Data * Insight * Action

Final Takeaways

By this point, you have practiced:

  • Communication
  • Storytelling
  • Presentation design
  • Constructive critique

These skills are essential in data science because insights only become valuable when audiences can understand them.

NoteFinal Thought

Great data presentations do more than display information — they help audiences understand, remember, and care.
